## Abstract

Game-based approaches are increasingly utilized as a tool for prosthesis training to create more interactive and accessible rehabilitation programs. This research aims to adopt these strategies for pediatric patients with upper limb differences through the development of virtual reality (VR) based systems.  The purpose of this project is to first develop and evaluate the usability of a new VR game that resembles the grasp and release behavior of the traditional Box and Block Test (BBT). We will then explore how grip strength and hand dimensions affect manual dexterity in VR training environments. 

The technical development of the new training game in Unity3D challenges users to use the VR system with electromyographic inputs to safely rescue as many troubled beachgoers from the ocean as possible within a time limit. The actuation of the grip mechanism by the electromyographic input is analogous to the process of the BBT. Replication of the kinesthetics and mechanics demonstrated in the BBT paired with interactive game elements is anticipated to produce similar training outcomes and promote transfer to real-world prosthesis use.

The experimental study will include data collection from individuals with full muscular control of their upper extremities. The project’s conclusion will expectantly reveal preliminary results on the usability, engagement, motivation and overall feasibility of integration of such systems into the Limbitless training schemes.

Future work aims to introduce the game to patients with upper limb difference and study the long-term effects of incorporating serious VR games into training, particularly in relation to impacts on prosthesis control.
